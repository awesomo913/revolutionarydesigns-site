Cacti from seed

Keep the seed history, use a suitable environment, and record what actually germinates.

Start with a traceable seed lot

Record the supplied name, seed parent, pollen parent if known, breeder, harvest year and lot number. For open-pollinated seed, keep the pollen parent unknown. Give seedlings individual numbers while preserving the lot reference. Seed from a named clone is not a cutting of that clone.

Do not infer a supplier’s quality or legal status from a group membership. Keep receipts and propagation records, and read the sourcing guide.

Prepare a small, labelled trial

Use a clean container and a suitable, fine-textured propagation medium. Moisten evenly without waterlogging. For the small-seeded species commonly grown under covers, surface sowing in bright indirect light is a useful starting method; obtain species-specific guidance for unfamiliar seed.

Keep covered containers out of direct sun and monitor temperature. A sealed box can overheat rapidly. Do not apply one bleach, peroxide or hot-water treatment to every cactus seed lot: treatment tolerance and contamination risk differ.

Light requirements are not identical

14 Peruvian species studied A 2024 germination experiment found 12 species responded positively to light, while two germinated in both light and darkness. The experiment included Trichocereus cuzcoensis and T. santaensis; it did not establish an exact optimum for every pachanoi clone.

Acta Botanica Mexicana · Light and 14 Peruvian cactus species (2024) ↗

Freshest is not always best

Ariocarpus kotschoubeyanus studied Research on seed retention found that stored seeds remaining on plants could germinate better than freshly produced seed under the tested conditions. This is a species-specific result, not a reason to age every seed lot deliberately.

Plant Species Biology · Seed retention in Ariocarpus kotschoubeyanus (2022) ↗

Raise seedlings by observation

Record sowing count, germination count and dates. Keep the medium appropriately moist during early establishment, inspect for algae, pests and collapsing seedlings, and adjust ventilation gradually. Avoid changing light, humidity and potting conditions abruptly at the same time.

If seedlings collapse, remove affected material and investigate the cause. Damping-off can involve multiple organisms; calling every collapse a fungus does not identify it. Pot on when plants and roots can tolerate handling, not simply when a calendar says six months.

Pollination and later identity

Record the cross before the flower closes, then keep fruit and seed lots separate. Self-compatibility varies; a finding in one species or population cannot settle every plant in the genus. A failed fruit can reflect compatibility, pollen condition, timing or growing conditions.

For grafting, use the grafting guide. Do not promise a universal tenfold growth increase or a fixed age at flowering.
