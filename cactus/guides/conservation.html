Conservation & responsible sourcing

Keep a record that can travel with the plant

Keep the received name, supplier, acquisition date, receipt, propagation method and any permits. Record a cutting chain separately from a seed-lot history. A supplier’s assertion should remain an attributed assertion until the supporting record is available.

Photographs and an unbroken history help, but appearance or price alone cannot prove that a plant was nursery-grown or wild-collected. A social group listing is not a legal or provenance certification.

Cultivated does not mean automatically exempt

There is no blanket CITES exemption for every cultivated cactus, artificially propagated plant or hybrid. Coverage depends on the listed taxon, material and relevant annotation. Artificially propagated Appendix-I material can have a lawful trade pathway under applicable conditions; “all cross-border trade is illegal” is also too broad.

Before a shipment, check the current CITES listing and the exporting and importing authorities’ requirements. Plant-health rules and domestic restrictions are separate questions. The US Fish & Wildlife Service provides a specific application for exporting artificially propagated plants.

Use conservation categories carefully

An IUCN category describes extinction risk; a CITES Appendix controls international trade. Neither is a general permission to possess, collect or ship a plant. Check the assessment date, listed taxon and applicable annotation when using either system.

Support populations and their context

Propagation can reduce demand for wild plants when paired with documented legal sourcing. It does not make every nursery purchase equivalent to saving one wild cactus. Habitat protection, population research and local and Indigenous stewardship remain important.

For Lophophora, read the Cactus Conservation Institute’s research. The Codex does not reproduce directions to vulnerable populations or endorse harvesting wild plants.

A practical acquisition checklist

  • What exact plant or seed lot is offered?
  • What evidence supports its source and propagation history?
  • Which names or parentage claims remain unverified?
  • What documentation must accompany this particular movement?
  • Can the plant be quarantined and inspected on arrival?
