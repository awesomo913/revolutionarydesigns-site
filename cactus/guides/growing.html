Choose the right starting group

Begin with the received botanical name and habitat group. Desert globular cacti, vigorous columnar cacti and tropical epiphytes should not inherit one identical care schedule. Haworthia and many other familiar succulents are not cacti.

Columnar cacti

Track active growth, root establishment and pot drying. Acclimate new arrivals to your light.

Small desert globulars

Allow room for the actual root system. Avoid a large volume of persistently wet mix around a small plant.

Epiphytic cacti

Schlumbergera and other rainforest cacti need a different moisture and light approach from desert species.

Grafted plants

Consider both partners. The rootstock influences water supply and cold response; the scion still has its own requirements.

RHS · Growing cacti and succulents ↗

Mix for air and predictable drying

Use drainage holes and a physically stable medium. Pumice, suitable grit and other coarse components can improve structure; organic components retain water and nutrients. Particle size, pot depth, root mass and airflow all affect drying.

Choose a recipe as a starting point, then make a small trial batch, wet it thoroughly, observe its drying in your actual pot, and adjust. A fast drain at the bottom does not prove that the root zone has dried.

Water by conditions

Check below the surface and compare pot weight. Established desert cacti generally need a drying interval; seedlings and epiphytes need separate decisions. Reduce irrigation when growth and water use slow, especially in cool conditions. A calendar reminder should trigger inspection, not automatic watering.

Investigate a plant that stays shrivelled despite wet media: damaged roots cannot be fixed simply by watering more. Likewise, yellowing or a dark spot is not enough to prove overwatering.

UC IPM · Cactus pests and disorders ↗

Light without a sudden shock

Increase exposure gradually after shipping, indoor growth or a seasonal move. Watch the newest growth and the sun-facing surface. Lamp distance or color temperature alone does not specify how much useful light reaches a plant; fixtures differ.

Summer greenhouse heat and winter frost involve exposure duration as well as temperature. The Codex does not give a universal freezing threshold for a species from an isolated survival story.

Nutrition: measure before adding more

Use a complete fertilizer appropriate to the plant and medium, following its label. Consider what the irrigation water and existing mix already supply. Record product, dilution and timing. More fertilizer is not automatically more vigor.

Calcium, magnesium and nitrogen form matter, but limestone, gypsum and Cal-Mag are not interchangeable. A symptom photograph cannot establish which nutrient is deficient. If problems persist, water, substrate and tissue testing can be more useful than repeatedly adding amendments. A fixed pH target or gypsum dose for every cactus is not established here.

Separate recovery from routine care

Inspect new arrivals before joining the collection. Keep a dated record of injuries, sprays and recovery. Use Cactus health for unexplained lesions and Pests for insects. Experiments should change one factor at a time on a small number of plants, with an untreated comparison where practical.

What the game teaches

Gritty Mix lets you practice observation, substrate choices and grafting decisions. Its growth multipliers, time scales and event probabilities are designed for play; they are not measured cultivation predictions.
