Althea: keep the evidence specific

GeeBee’s public offerings include Althea. That confirms a primary grower listing; the offering alone does not establish a complete pedigree, a GB code, or equivalence to another named plant. Those details should remain unassigned unless separately documented.

Grower practice and a treatment trial are different

In a public seasonal note, GeeBee describes outdoor cultivation, sulfur on cut ends and gypsum foliar use. That is an attributed practice report. It is not a controlled demonstration that gypsum cures cactus infections, and it does not establish a safe application rate for every plant.

Turn observations into useful questions

What changed first?

Watering, root damage, temperature, light, nutrition or a spray can precede visible injury. Record the sequence.

What organism was found?

Inspect or test before turning “spot,” “rot” or “brooming” into a pathogen name.

Which host was studied?

Keep a result on Opuntia, dragon fruit or grafted Gymnocalycium attached to that host.

What would test the idea?

Repeated observations, an appropriate comparison and laboratory identification are stronger than an anecdotal recovery.

This question framework is the Codex’s editorial synthesis. It is not presented as a quotation or an endorsement by GeeBee.

Three distinctions worth keeping

  • Susceptibility is not diagnosis. Cultural stress may make infection more likely; it does not identify the pathogen.
  • A nutrient is not automatically a cure. Calcium-related physiology does not establish that every soft-rot problem is caused by calcium deficiency.
  • A better-looking plant is not a clean test. Symptom improvement does not by itself exclude persistent infection.
