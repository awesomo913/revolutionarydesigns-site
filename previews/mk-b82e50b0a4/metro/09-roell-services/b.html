Your play, in one breath

You're not selling an idea — you're handing them a website that already exists with their name, town, trade, a working call button and a quote form. That's the whole edge. Let them look, then leave it with them.

  • Local set = drive-to. Knock, catch them at the shop, hand over the phone.
  • Metro set = mostly phone. Higher volume, call and text the link.
  • Lead with the design flagged below, but the compare page lets them pick.
  • Anchor price to one job: "one roof / driveway / deck covers it."

Objections, one-liners

  • "I'm on Facebook / Google already." → You don't own it and can't rank it. A real site shows up first and sends them to your phone.
  • "All my work's word of mouth." → This just catches the ones who look you up before they call.
  • "How much?" → Give your number plainly, then anchor to one job.
  • "No time." → That's the point — you do nothing. I build it, you look, I put it live.
  • "Is that really my site?" → It's a sample of what yours would look like; photos + reviews get swapped for yours.

Don't get caught out

  • Reviews & job photos are placeholders — say so; swap for real before go-live.
  • Name / trade / phone come from public listings — confirm on the spot, fix if wrong.

"One more thing. When somebody calls you after hours right now, what happens? … Right. I can put a thing on your line that picks up every time, answers the basic stuff, and texts you the name and number before they've hung up. Want to hear it? Here — call this."

Objections

  • "I answer my own phone." → Not at 8pm on a Saturday you don't - and that's when half of these calls come in. This only picks up what you were already missing.
  • "Won't it sound like a robot?" → Let them hear it. Call the demo on the spot and hand them the phone - that's the whole close.
  • "What if it says something wrong?" → It only knows what we put in it - your services, your area, your hours. It books and takes messages; it never quotes a job.
  • "I'll just get an answering service." → Those charge per call and read from a card. This one knows your trade and your town, and it texts you the lead before they've hung up.

Don't oversell

  • Sell this after the site is signed - never instead of it. The site is the product; this is the add-on.
  • It answers, qualifies and books. It does not diagnose, price a job, or promise a tech by a certain hour. Say that out loud.
  • Minutes are metered on your side - don't promise "unlimited." Heavy months get a conversation, not a surprise invoice.
  • Don't claim it replaces an office person. It covers the hours nobody is there.
  • If they want calls recorded, check your state's consent rules first - they vary and it is their liability as much as yours.
You are not selling "AI." You are selling the calls they are already losing.

Who to go after first

  • Tier 1 — high ticket, high miss rate. Plumbing, septic, HVAC, electrical, roofing, garage doors. One missed call is a job worth hundreds to five figures.
  • Tier 2 — booking driven. Dental, vet, salons, storage, gyms. Steady volume, after-hours booking is the win.
  • Tier 3 — your warm lane. Nurseries, garden centres, landscapers. You speak the language already.

The wedge is after-hours. Most shops are closed 5pm–8am and weekends — over half the week. You are not replacing their office person (scary, gets a no). You are covering the hours nobody is there.

Five ways to sell it

  • 1. Website upsell — warmest. Every site you build and every Site Care client is already a buyer. Start here before any cold work.
  • 2. Demo first. Build their agent from their own website before you pitch: "I built your shop a receptionist — call it." Beats any script.
  • 3. After-hours audit. Call their line at 8pm, note what happens. That is the pitch. (Check consent law before recording anything.)
  • 4. Content. Film the build. Nobody else in this niche can — it is your moat and it works while you sleep.
  • 5. Local. Chamber, BNI, trade suppliers. These owners buy from people they met.

Pricing rules

  • $500 setup + $199/mo. Setup fee plus retainer — never per-minute to the client, or they fixate on the meter and you eat the overage.
  • Anchor to one job: "what is your average ticket?" → "this pays for itself if it catches one a month." Insurance, not expense.
  • Cap included minutes with a defined overage so a viral month cannot sink you.
  • Your cost ~$10-35/mo — margin ~$165-190/mo. Stacked with Site Care: $298/mo.

Qualify in one question

"What happens when somebody calls you at 7pm?"
Voicemail → you have a customer. "My wife answers" → walk. Skip anyone who already answers reliably, and anyone with almost no inbound calls.

The red line — do not cross

No ringless voicemail blasts. No scraped call lists. The FCC ruled in Nov 2022 that ringless voicemail is a "call" under the TCPA needing prior express consent. B2B is not exempt on cell numbers. Damages are $500 per call, trebled to $1,500 if willful, uncapped — 500 drops is a $250k–750k exposure. The guru videos selling this method are affiliate funnels. Inbound and demos only.

How it actually gets built

  • Phase 1 — web widget, no phone. ElevenLabs agent (customer-service template) embedded on their site. No phone number means no TCPA surface at all.
  • Phase 2 — the phone. Link a Twilio number once Phase 1 has earned it. This is where the real money is, because this is where the loss is.
  • Phase 3 — the reel. Film it. Demo asset and funnel in one.
  • Knowledge base = their services, area, hours. It books and takes messages; it never quotes a job.
