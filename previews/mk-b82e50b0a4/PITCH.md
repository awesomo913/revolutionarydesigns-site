# The pitch — how to turn these mockups into signed jobs

You're walking in (or calling) with a website already built for *their* business — their name, their
town, their trade, a working call button and quote form. That's the whole edge: you're not selling
an idea, you're showing them a thing that already exists.

Two lead sets are in this folder:

- **`local/`** — 20 businesses within **20 miles of 41035** (Dry Ridge, Walton, Union, Independence,
  Crittenden, Williamstown, Corinth, Morning View, Sparta). These are the **drive-to** list — knock
  on the door, catch them at the shop, hand them your phone.
- **`metro/`** — 20 across the wider 50-mile Cincinnati / N. Kentucky metro (Hamilton, Middletown,
  Lexington, Lawrenceburg). Higher volume, mostly a **phone** list.

Open `index.html` to see both. Each business has a **`compare.html`** that loads all three designs
side by side.

## Why these 20 (local set)

Every one of them either has **no website at all** or only a **directory placeholder** (the
auto-generated Yellow-Pages-style page they don't own and can't edit). That's the opening line:
someone searching their trade in their town finds competitors first, or finds a page that isn't
really theirs. They're also **established, high-ticket trades** — roofing, paving, concrete, HVAC,
septic, fencing, pole barns — where one closed job pays for the site many times over.

They range from **right in Dry Ridge (0 mi)** out to about **17 miles**. Grab-and-go order by
distance is in `local/index.html` (each card shows the mileage).

## The six directions — lead with the one that fits the trade

Every business now comes in **six** looks (a–f), and **every page uses real trade photos** (a
roof photo for roofers, a driveway for pavers, and so on). The photos are AI-generated stand-ins,
clearly sample imagery — swap in the owner's real photos before anything goes live.

| Design | Feel | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **A — On Call** | Dark, urgent, giant tap-to-call | Emergencies & "call now" trades: roofing, plumbing, septic, HVAC, garage doors |
| **B — Trusted Local** | Calm, warm, badges + FAQ | Homeowner-vetting trades: fencing, gutters, painting, handyman, landscaping |
| **C — Showcase** | Cream editorial, serif, portfolio | Design-led / high-ticket: pole barns, concrete, remodels, custom builds |
| **D — Field Photo** | Bold full-bleed photo hero, teal, sticky call bar | Photo-forward trades that sell on the work itself: roofing, paving, decks, concrete |
| **E — Catalog** | Bright, clean service-card grid with photos | Multi-service shops: handyman, remodel, GC, landscaping, home solutions |
| **F — Story** | Warm serif photo-essay, unhurried | High-trust, relationship trades: custom builds, fencing, painting, long-established names |

You don't have to pick for them — the compare page lets *them* point at the one they like. But walk
in leaning toward the one above so the first thing they see already feels right. A/D read boldest,
B/E read friendliest and most organized, C/F read most premium.

## The offer (fill in your own numbers)

> A real **5-page website they own** — home, services, service area, about, contact — plus their
> **Google Business Profile** set up so they show up when someone nearby searches their trade. Mobile-first,
> loads fast, working quote form and click-to-call.

Pricing is yours to set. Common shape for this market: **a one-time build** (e.g. $___), or **build +
a small monthly** for hosting, edits, and keeping the Google profile current (e.g. $___ /mo). Decide
before you walk in; don't quote off the cuff.

## What to say

**In person (30 seconds):**
> "Hey — I build websites for local trades, and I actually already put one together for
> [Business]. Mind if I show you real quick? … This is your name, your number, works on a phone.
> Right now if somebody Googles '[trade] in [town]' they don't find you — they find the other guys.
> I can get you a real site you own and set up your Google so you turn up first. Want me to leave
> this with you?"

**Phone / voicemail:**
> "Hi, this is [you] — I build websites for local contractors. I made a sample site for [Business]
> and wanted to show it to you; no cost to look. Give me a call back at [number] and I'll text you
> the link. Thanks."

**Text / email follow-up:**
> "Here's that mockup I made for [Business]: [link]. It's just a sample — your real one would have
> your photos and reviews. Happy to walk through it whenever. — [you]"

## Objections, handled

- **"I've got a Facebook page / I'm on Google already."** → "That's a great start, but you don't
  own it and you can't rank a Facebook page. A real site is the thing that shows up first and sends
  people straight to your phone."
- **"I get all my work by word of mouth."** → "Perfect — this just catches the ones who look you up
  before they call. Most people check a site before they trust a roof or a driveway to somebody."
- **"How much?"** → give your number plainly, then anchor to one job: "One [roof/driveway/deck]
  covers it."
- **"I don't have time for this."** → "That's the point — you do nothing. I build it, you look it
  over, I put it live. I even set up the Google side."
- **"Is that really my site?"** → "It's a sample I built to show you what yours would look like —
  the layout's real, the photos and reviews get swapped for yours once we go."

---

## Honesty notes (so you don't get caught out)

- The **reviews and job photos in every mockup are placeholders**, clearly labeled as samples. Don't
  present them as real. Swap in the owner's real photos/reviews before anything goes live.
- Business **facts** (name, trade, town, phone) come from public listings and can be slightly off —
  confirm the name and number with the owner; fix on the spot if wrong.
- A couple of names were tidied for display (e.g. "Hanna's Painting," "J3 Heating"). Ask them how
  they want it written.
