How to read the labels

Policy conflict means the reviewed evidence visibly conflicts with a current published requirement. High risk means a material submission risk or missing required evidence. Medium means a narrower issue or platform-guidance gap. Note is a best-practice or consistency recommendation. These labels are the reviewer's scoped judgment, not a prediction from Google.

Detailed findings

The five highest-severity findings are expanded below to demonstrate the format. This public sample keeps the remaining seven in the summary table; a paid report expands every applicable finding with its evidence, basis, correction, and recheck condition.

Policy conflict

Data safety declaration understates what the app collects

Evidence observed: the supplied Data safety draft states "No data collected." The supplied SDK inventory names Northbeam Analytics, and one first-launch traffic observation showed a request to the vendor's test endpoint containing values that matched the test device's resettable advertising identifier and an app-scoped installation identifier.

Why it matters: Google requires declarations to include user data transmitted off device by third-party SDKs and states that a discrepancy between app behavior and the declaration may lead to enforcement. This observation establishes a mismatch in the tested flow; it does not establish every data type, purpose, recipient role, or server-side use.

Requirement cited: Google Play Data safety, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469

Recommended correction: inventory the SDK's transmitted data and purposes, confirm from the vendor agreement whether the vendor acts as a service provider or another third party, and update the Data safety answers for "Device or other IDs" as applicable. Do not infer the sharing answer from this traffic observation alone.

Recheck condition: provide the revised Data safety draft and the vendor-role evidence used to answer the collection, purpose, and sharing questions.

Policy conflict

No in-app path to request account deletion

Evidence observed: the reviewed build enables account creation and email/password sign-in. No tested screen, menu item, or settings option initiates account deletion, and the supplied Data safety deletion-web-URL field is blank.

Why it matters: Google says an app that enables account creation must provide both an in-app path to request deletion of the app account and associated data and a web resource where users can make that request without reinstalling the app. The in-app path may link to the web resource.

Requirement cited: Google Play account deletion policy, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111

Recommended correction: add a prominent in-app path under account settings that initiates account and associated-data deletion, publish a functional deletion-request web resource, and enter that URL in the Data safety form. Disclose any permitted retention rather than promising immediate erasure if that is not true.

Recheck condition: supply a new build in which the request can be initiated and the final deletion URL can be reached. Backend erasure remains outside this review.

Policy conflict

Store screenshots show a feature that isn't in this build

Observed: two of the six submitted screenshots show a "Premium offline layer" map style with terrain shading. The submitted build has no such layer; the only offline map style available is the standard trail overlay.

Why it matters: store listings are required to represent the app accurately. A screenshot showing a feature the build doesn't have reads as a misleading claim, independent of whether it was intentional.

Requirement cited: Google Play policy on deceptive behavior and misleading claims. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17006354

Recommended correction: remove the two screenshots showing the Premium layer, or hold submission until that layer ships and have the new build rechecked against replacement screenshots.

Recheck condition: provide the final screenshot set and, if the feature is retained, a build in which the shown layer can be reached.

High risk

Subscription price and renewal terms aren't shown before purchase

Observed: tapping "Go Premium" opens the platform billing sheet directly. Nothing in the app screen before that tap states the price, billing period, or that the subscription auto-renews.

Why it matters: Google's subscriptions policy requires clear disclosure of the offer terms, cost, billing frequency, automatic renewal, and other material information without requiring an additional action to review it.

Requirement cited: Google Play subscriptions policy, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533

Recommended correction: before the purchase button, show the localized price, billing cycle, automatic-renewal language, benefits, and a clear path or explanation for managing and canceling the subscription.

Recheck condition: provide a new build and test product configuration so the full pre-purchase screen can be compared with the billing sheet.

High risk

Content-rating answers don't match a user-generated-content feed

Observed: the content rating questionnaire was answered "no user interaction" and "no user-generated content." The app has a public "Trail Notes" feed where any signed-in user can post a text comment visible to other users.

Why it matters: Google requires accurate content-rating answers and requires apps with user-generated content to implement applicable safeguards, including in-app reporting and blocking paths.

Requirements cited: Google Play Content Ratings policy, support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898843; Google Play User Generated Content policy, support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937.

Recommended correction: answer the questionnaire to reflect online interaction and user-generated content, and add accessible report and block paths plus the other moderation controls applicable to this feed.

Recheck condition: provide the revised questionnaire and a new build in which the report and block flows can be completed with test accounts.

Not evaluated

Stated plainly, this review did not and could not establish the following from the materials supplied:

  • Server-side data handlingWhat Northbeam's backend does with the identifiers it receives, how long it retains them, and who else it shares them with. That lives outside the client build.
  • Backend retention and deletion timelinesWhether a deletion request is actually honored on the server within any stated window.
  • Source-code completenessNo source code was supplied. The SDK inventory and observed traffic can reveal inconsistencies, but they do not establish every dormant code path, dependency behavior, or transmitted data type.
  • Network behavior outside the observed flowOnly one unpinned first-launch session was observed. Certificate-pinned traffic, later lifecycle events, other network states, and server-to-server transfers were not inspected.
  • Additional billing lifecycle statesOnly the named license-tester reinstall/entitlement scenario was exercised. Renewals, grace periods, holds, refunds, upgrades, regional pricing, and other purchase states were not tested.
  • Other devices, roles, locales, and regionsThis review used the one emulator, locale, and standard user role named in the scope. Behavior on other manufacturers, OS versions, screen sizes, account roles, languages, or storefront regions was not tested.
  • Anything not present in the supplied build or materialsFeatures on a roadmap, planned copy changes, or a newer build produced after this report was delivered.
  • Future policy changesBoth stores update enforcement and requirements on their own schedule. This report reflects the rules in effect on the report date above.

What this sample does not claim

A “policy conflict” label is not a prediction of approval or rejection, and clearing every item in this report is not an approval guarantee. It means the supplied evidence visibly conflicted with the cited requirement within the stated scope. Google can raise issues outside that evidence and scope. This review narrows visible submission gaps; it does not certify compliance or speak for the store's review team.
