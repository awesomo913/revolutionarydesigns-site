Detailed findings

The five findings below are the highest severity in this report, written out in full. The remaining seven are captured in the summary table above.

Blocker

Data safety declaration understates what the app collects

Observed: the submitted Data safety form states "No data collected." The build includes an analytics SDK initialized in Application.onCreate that reads and transmits the Android advertising ID and a device identifier on first launch.

Why it matters: a mismatch between the declared Data safety answers and actual collection is one of the most common causes of a policy rejection or a later account-level enforcement action, and it isn't something a source scan alone can confirm without seeing the live network calls.

Requirement cited: Google Play Data safety, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469

Recommended fix: update the Data safety form to disclose device or advertising identifier collection under "Device or other IDs," specify the purpose (analytics), and confirm whether the data is shared with the SDK vendor.

Blocker

No in-app path to delete an account

Observed: the app lets a user create an account and sign in with email and password. No screen, menu item, or settings option initiates account deletion from inside the app, and the linked privacy policy does not name an alternative deletion method.

Why it matters: apps that support account creation are required to offer an accessible way to request deletion of the account and associated data, either in-app or via a clearly linked web path meeting the same requirement. Missing this is a routine rejection reason.

Requirement cited: Google Play account deletion policy, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111

Recommended fix: add an in-app "Delete account" action under account settings that removes the account and associated data, or, at minimum, a clearly linked web request path that satisfies the same policy, and confirm the timeline for data removal in the linked policy text.

Blocker

Store screenshots show a feature that isn't in this build

Observed: two of the six submitted screenshots show a "Premium offline layer" map style with terrain shading. The submitted build has no such layer; the only offline map style available is the standard trail overlay.

Why it matters: store listings are required to represent the app accurately. A screenshot showing a feature the build doesn't have reads as a misleading claim, independent of whether it was intentional.

Requirement cited: Google Play policy on deceptive behavior and misleading claims. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077, general policy center: play.google.com/about/developer-content-policy

Recommended fix: either remove the two screenshots showing the Premium layer or hold submission until that layer ships in the reviewed build, then resubmit screenshots that match exactly what a user will see.

High

Subscription price and renewal terms aren't shown before purchase

Observed: tapping "Go Premium" opens the platform billing sheet directly. Nothing in the app screen before that tap states the price, billing period, or that the subscription auto-renews.

Why it matters: subscription terms have to be clear to the user before they commit to a purchase, not only inside the platform's own billing sheet. Reviewers check for this in the app's own UI.

Requirement cited: Google Play subscriptions policy, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504

Recommended fix: add a pre-purchase screen or label showing the price, billing cycle, and a plain statement that the subscription renews automatically until canceled, before the button that opens the billing sheet.

High

Age rating answers don't match a user-generated content feed

Observed: the content rating questionnaire was answered "no user interaction" and "no user-generated content." The app has a public "Trail Notes" feed where any signed-in user can post a text comment visible to other users.

Why it matters: the age rating a user sees is generated from these answers. A public comments feed with no reporting or blocking tools is a materially different risk profile than the questionnaire currently reflects.

Requirement cited: content rating questionnaire guidance, published requirement. support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455

Recommended fix: re-answer the questionnaire to reflect the user-generated content feed, and add a report and block path to the feed itself before resubmitting.

Not evaluated

Stated plainly, this review did not and could not establish the following from the materials supplied:

  • Server-side data handlingWhat Northbeam's backend does with the identifiers it receives, how long it retains them, and who else it shares them with. That lives outside the client build.
  • Backend retention and deletion timelinesWhether a deletion request is actually honored on the server within any stated window.
  • Device-specific crashesThis review used one test device and one emulator profile. Behavior on other manufacturers, OS versions, or screen sizes was not tested.
  • Anything not present in the supplied build or materialsFeatures on a roadmap, planned copy changes, or a newer build produced after this report was delivered.
  • Future policy changesBoth stores update enforcement and requirements on their own schedule. This report reflects the rules in effect on the report date above.

What this sample does not claim

No identified blocker here is a prediction of approval or rejection, and clearing every item in this report is not an approval guarantee. Apple and Google can raise issues that fall outside the materials a developer supplies, including ones that only surface once a human reviewer opens the live app. This report narrows the mechanical and judgment-based gaps visible from the supplied build and listing; it does not and cannot speak for either store's review team.
