One review, three surfaces

Most preventable submission problems are contradictions between these surfaces, not a single missing checkbox.

1. The runnable app

Onboarding, login, permissions, purchases, restore and cancellation paths, account deletion, links, dead ends, and the other agreed flows.

2. The store package

Listing claims, screenshots, preview video, pricing language, reviewer notes, support links, and the build you intend to submit.

3. The declarations

Privacy label or Data Safety answers, age-rating answers, privacy-policy claims, account-deletion information, and store-specific disclosures.

A full review requires a runnable build

Runnable build: Revo can independently open the app and navigate the agreed screens and flows. That supports a full in-scope readiness assessment.

Video only: if a build genuinely cannot be shared, AppShield can consider a limited materials-consistency review. A recording can show what you chose to record; it cannot prove that links work, login succeeds, permissions behave correctly, purchases complete, or unrecorded paths exist. Those items are marked Not Evaluated, and the report is labeled limited rather than presented as a full build review.

How evidence becomes a finding

  • Observe the agreed flowThe report names the build, device or test environment, screen, step, and visible behavior relied on.
  • Compare it with your submitted materialsThe relevant listing claim, screenshot, declaration, policy text, or reviewer note is quoted or described beside the observation.
  • Check the published requirementStore-policy findings cite an official Apple or Google source current on the report date. Engineering recommendations are labeled as recommendations, not store law.
  • State the boundaryMissing evidence is never silently counted as a pass. The report says what was observed, what remains a risk, and what could not be evaluated.

What the agreed flows can cover

  • Store listing claims vs actual app behaviorWhether what the listing promises matches the supplied runnable build.
  • Screenshot accuracyWhether screenshots reflect the current build and don't mislead.
  • Privacy labels or Data Safety answers vs available evidenceDeclarations are compared with permissions, observable behavior, and the SDK/data inventory you supply. Server-side processing, dormant paths, retention, and legal compliance are not verified.
  • Age-rating answers vs visible contentWhether the questionnaire answers match what's on screen.
  • In-app purchases and subscriptionsVisible pricing, entitlement, restoration or management, and cancellation paths when sandbox or test access permits. Untestable billing states are marked Not Evaluated.
  • Account creation and deletionWhether a user-facing deletion-request path exists and can be initiated. Backend erasure and retention are not verified without separate evidence.
  • Login and reviewer accessWhether a reviewer can actually get into the app to test it.
  • User-generated-content safeguards, where applicableWhether visible report, block, and moderation paths exist and can be reached—not whether moderation is effective.
  • Broken links, placeholder content, dead endsThe kind of thing that stalls a review cycle for no good reason.
  • Store-specific declarations and reviewer notesWhether what you'll submit to the reviewer matches what's true.
