Android developer verification: what it actually requires (2026–2027)

Google is rolling out identity verification for Android app developers. Every date, fee, and requirement on this page is quoted or paraphrased directly from Google's own developer-verification pages, linked in the sources section. Nothing here is guessed.

Which account do you need

Google offers two account types. Which one you need depends on how you plan to distribute, not on how technical you are.

Government IDNot requiredRequired
Who it's forStudents, teachers, and hobbyists — sharing with family, friends, or a classroom, no commercial intentAnyone publishing for wide or commercial distribution

Organizations distributing apps register as an organization and need a D-U-N-S number (see below). Individual developers going for Full Distribution use their own government ID instead.

What to prepare

If you're registering as an organization

  • D-U-N-S numberA free nine-digit business identifier from Dun & Bradstreet. Google's FAQ: "The process can take up to 28 days so you should make preparations to obtain a D-U-N-S number." Start this well before your deadline.
  • Legal entity documentationWhatever your organization uses to prove it is the entity it claims to be, matching the D-U-N-S record.
  • A Google Payments profileLinked with the organization's legal name and address.

If you're registering as an individual

  • Government-issued IDRequired for a Full Distribution account. Not required for Limited Distribution.
  • A Google Account with 2-Step Verification enabled
  • A Google Payments profileWith your legal name and address.
  • A contact email addressGoogle uses this to reach you if needed; it is not made public.

Either way

  • A secure key-management plan for your app-signing keysGoogle's FAQ warns: "If you lose your signing key you won't be able to register your packages."
  • A list of every package name and form factor you distributeIf you distribute on Google Play, apps across all your form factors must be registered.

What verification does NOT require

It does not require your app's source code or functionality. Google's FAQ: "Android Developer Verification does not collect information about the app content or functionality so no confidential app content or purpose needs to be shared." Verification checks who the developer is, not what the app does.

It does not affect ADB installs during development. Google's FAQ: "As a developer, you are free to install apps without verification with ADB." The 24-hour waiting period in the advanced flow also does not apply to ADB installs.

It does not close Android to alternative app stores. Google: "Android remains an open ecosystem where you distribute through a variety of channels you choose and install software from multiple sources." Users can still install unverified apps via a one-time advanced-flow setup.

It does not apply to enterprise apps on managed devices. Google's FAQ: "Apps distributed through your organization store, on managed devices, won't need to complete the verification requirements since your IT admin has vetted them." Google still recommends registering them for a smoother install experience.

It does not apply in sanctioned countries. Google's FAQ: "Developers in sanctioned countries will be excluded from Android developer verification checks."

FAQ

What happens if I don't verify by the deadline?
In the four initial regions, on participating stores, your apps get blocked from installing on certified devices running Android 7+. Outside those regions and stores, nothing changes in September 2026, but Google recommends verifying before the 2027 global rollout.
Does verification require a government ID?
Only for a Full Distribution account. The free Limited Distribution account (students, teachers, hobbyists, up to 20 devices) needs no government ID and no fee.
What is a D-U-N-S number and do I need one?
A free nine-digit business identifier from Dun & Bradstreet, required if you register as an organization. It can take up to 28 days to get, so start early if your org doesn't already have one.
Can I still sideload apps from developers who haven't verified?
Yes, through the advanced flow: enable developer mode, confirm you're not being coached, restart and reauthenticate, wait 24 hours, then confirm with biometrics or a PIN. It's a one-time setup per device.
Does this apply to my app if I only distribute through my own organization's store on managed devices?
No. Google's FAQ exempts apps distributed through an organization store on managed devices, since IT has already vetted them, though registering is still recommended.
What if I lose my app-signing key?
Google's FAQ is blunt about this: without it, you won't be able to register your packages. Use a secure key-management solution before this becomes a problem.
