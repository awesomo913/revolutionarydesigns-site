One review · three surfaces

Your submission is a system, not a checklist.

Reviewers see the app, the storefront, and your declarations together. AppShield does the same within a clearly recorded test scope.

01

The runnable build

What a reviewer can actually reach and understand in the supplied build.

  • Onboarding and core flows
  • Account creation and deletion path
  • Pricing, entitlements, and management paths
  • Permissions, errors, and reviewer access
02

The store package

What your listing promises before anyone installs the app.

  • Name, subtitle, and description
  • Screenshots and previews
  • Support and privacy links
  • Release notes and reviewer instructions
03

The declarations

Whether your draft answers agree with the evidence you supplied.

  • Privacy and Data Safety answers
  • Age-rating questionnaire
  • SDK and data inventory
  • Purchases, ads, and user content

Built with AI?

Do not ask the builder to grade its own work.

Fast builders are excellent at producing screens and code. Submission risk often lives in the seams: a permission the privacy answer forgot, an old screenshot, or reviewer access that fails outside your own device.

✓Evidence over confidence. Findings name what was observed and where.
✓Sources over folklore. Policy claims link to current Apple or Google documentation.
✓Limits over theater. Anything not tested or not provable is marked that way.
